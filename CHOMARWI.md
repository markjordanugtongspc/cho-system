# DOMAIN CONTEXT: City Health Office (CHO) - Marawi City, BARMM

## 1. Overview & Identity
The **City Health Office (CHO) of Marawi** serves as the primary public healthcare agency for the Islamic City of Marawi, located in the Province of Lanao del Sur. It operates under the executive governance of the **City Government of Marawi** and is technically and programmatically guided by the **Ministry of Health – Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (MOH-BARMM)**, in alignment with national health standards from the Department of Health (DOH).

The office is responsible for delivering comprehensive, equitable, and culturally responsive basic healthcare services to the city’s 96 barangays. It serves long-time residents, business operators, students, and families residing in post-conflict resettlement areas.

---

## 2. Core Mandate & Purpose
The primary purpose of the Marawi City Health Office is to safeguard public health through prevention, clinical intervention, disease monitoring, and health education. Its core objectives include:
- Providing accessible, free or low-cost primary medical and dental care at the community level.
- Protecting the population against vaccine-preventable diseases through routine and supplementary immunization.
- Monitoring environmental health, food hygiene, and water safety standards across local establishments.
- Managing public health emergencies, endemic outbreaks, and post-crisis health interventions.
- Serving as the grassroots foundation for Universal Health Care (UHC) integration within BARMM.

---

## 3. What the City Health Office Does (Core Services & Programs)

### Primary Clinical & Diagnostic Care
- **Outpatient Consultations:** General checkups, clinical diagnoses, first-aid treatment, minor surgical procedures, and prescription of essential medicines.
- **Laboratory & Diagnostic Testing:** Basic urinalysis, fecalysis, complete blood count (CBC), blood typing, sputum microscopy/GeneXpert, and rapid diagnostic tests for infectious diseases.
- **Dental Services:** Dental examinations, cleanings, oral health counseling, and tooth extractions.
- **Pharmacy & Medicine Dispensing:** Free provision of government-procured maintenance drugs, antibiotics, vitamins, and medical supplies to indigent patients.

### Maternal, Child Health & Nutrition (MCHN)
- **Antenatal & Postnatal Care:** Routine check-ups for pregnant mothers, tracking gestational health, birth plan coordination, iron/folic acid distribution, and postpartum recovery monitoring.
- **Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI):** Routine vaccination for infants and children against tuberculosis, hepatitis B, polio, diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, measles, and rubella.
- **Nutritional Interventions:** Community-wide nutrition assessments (*Operation Timbang Plus*), monitoring stunting and wasting in young children, and running targeted supplementary feeding programs.
- **Family Planning:** Reproductive health counseling, family planning education, and distribution of culturally sensitive maternal healthcare supplies.

### Communicable Disease Prevention & Control
- **Tuberculosis (TB-DOTS) Program:** Active case-finding, free sputum testing, diagnostic evaluation, and daily Directly Observed Treatment Short-Course (DOTS) management.
- **Vector-Borne & Waterborne Disease Control:** Larvicidal treatments, indoor residual spraying, rapid response to Dengue fever spikes, and monitoring for water-borne illnesses like cholera and typhoid.
- **Animal Bite Treatment Center (ABTC):** Post-exposure rabies prophylaxis (anti-rabies vaccinations and wound care) for dog and cat bites.
- **Epidemiological Surveillance:** Real-time logging of notifiable diseases, tracking cluster outbreaks in barangays, and transmitting health intelligence to MOH-BARMM.

### Non-Communicable Diseases (Lifestyle Health)
- **PhilPEN Interventions:** Cardiovascular risk assessments, blood sugar screening (diabetes), blood pressure checks (hypertension), and lifestyle coaching to address prevalent lifestyle conditions.

### Environmental Health & Sanitation
- **Health Certificates (Working Permits):** Mandatory physical screenings, stool examinations, and chest X-ray validations for food handlers, service workers, and commercial staff.
- **Sanitary Permits & Inspections:** Inspecting local eateries, markets, lodging houses, and public facilities to verify compliance with the Code on Sanitation of the Philippines (P.D. 856).
- **Water Source Monitoring:** Regular potability testing and water chlorination assistance across deep wells, refilling stations, and municipal water connections.

### Medico-Legal & Vital Registration Assistance
- **Medical Clearances:** Issuance of official medical certificates for employment, school admission, travel, sports activities, and government assistance applications.
- **Civil Registry Coordination:** Reviewing, certifying, and reporting causes of death, home births, and vital health events to the local civil registrar.

## 4. Local Realities & Specific Operational Context

### Post-Siege & Transitional Community Health
A major aspect of Marawi CHO's ongoing work involves attending to families affected by the 2017 siege. The office coordinates outreach missions and mobile health clinics to:
- **Transitional and Permanent Resettlement Sites:** Including areas such as Sagonsongan, Dulay, Kilala, and other designated shelter communities where primary facility infrastructure is still evolving.
- **Psychosocial and Mental Health:** Facilitating community-based mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) initiatives alongside regional humanitarian partners.

### Referral Network
While the City Health Office manages primary, preventive, and outpatient care, patients requiring specialized surgical, intensive, or advanced inpatient treatment are referred up the local health network, primarily to **Amai Pakpak Medical Center (APMC)**, the region's main tertiary government teaching hospital located within Marawi City.

### Cultural Integration
Public health strategies, health promotions, and maternal care delivered by the CHO are adapted to Islamic traditions, Halal compliance, local cultural norms of the Meranaw population, and local language needs to ensure trust and high community participation.
